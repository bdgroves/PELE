How to Read the Sawtooth — The Inflate-Fountain-Deflate Cycle

The UWD tiltmeter near Uēkahuna on the caldera rim is your best friend. It measures ground tilt in microradians (μrad) — one microradian is like tilting a kilometer-long beam by one millimeter. Here's what to watch for:

📈 RAMP UP (Inflation) — Magma filling the shallow reservoir. The ground swells and the tilt line climbs over days to weeks. This is the pause between episodes; the taller the ramp, the more magma is stored.
🔥 PRECURSORY OVERFLOWS — Lava spills from the vents as the magma column pushes to the surface. Small dome fountains and spattering. The pot is starting to boil over. Overflows can pause during brief deflation events, then resume when inflation returns.
⬇️ CLIFF (Deflation) — The fountaining episode begins. Rapid tilt drop as the reservoir empties through the vents. This happens fast — Episode 43 recorded 33.9 μrad of deflation in about 9 hours, and its south vent fountain reached 1,770 ft, the record for this eruption.
🔄 IMMEDIATE REBOUND — Episode ends abruptly. Inflation restarts immediately as new magma refills from below. The cycle begins again.

The full record (left) shows every episode since December 2024, each ramp and cliff one pause-and-fountain cycle. As pauses lengthened from days to weeks, the ramps grew taller. HVO has used the inflation rate and accumulated tilt to forecast windows for the next episode.

The pattern can break, and the plots show it when it does. After Episode 54 (Aug 25, 2026) the pause ran past every earlier one; in mid-September six new vents erupted lava flows without fountaining; and on the evening of September 30 the summit tiltmeters recorded rapid changes that HVO read as an east–west crack filling with magma under Halemaʻumaʻu, with a swarm of shallow earthquakes. Watch the week and month tilt plots for what comes next.

Kīlauea Episodic Fountaining Log

Since December 23, 2024, Kīlauea has erupted in a distinctive episodic pattern — short-lived lava fountaining from two vents in Halema'uma'u, separated by inflationary pauses. This style was last seen in the 1980s.
